A Button on the Screen

Today you make a button that sits on the screen. The player taps it, and it does something.

Karibu back, Builder! 🐦

So far you made things in the 3D world. Blocks. Lava. Coins. But games also have things on the screen. A score at the top. A "Play" button. A health bar. Today Fundi shows you how to draw a button right on the screen. The player taps it, and your code runs. This is called a GUI. Twende!

1What is a GUI

GUI: things drawn ON the screen

Look at any game on a phone. The buttons, the score, the words on top. Those are not in the 3D world. They float on top of the screen, like stickers on your phone glass. That is a GUI. Say it "goo-ee". It means the buttons and words you see on the screen.

🧱 3D world: in the game

  4. Now you can tell them apart.

Screen things float on top. World things stay in the world. 🔘

2Make the button

Add a ScreenGui, then a TextButton

Screen things live in a place called StarterGui. First you add a holder called a ScreenGui. A ScreenGui is just a box that holds screen things. Then you put a TextButton inside it. A TextButton is a button with words on it.

  2. Put your mouse on it. Click the + sign.

🍌 BRAIN SNACK: GUI is on the screen, blocks are in the world

Your blocks live in the 3D world. You walk up to them. A GUI is different. It sits flat on the screen, in front of everything, like a sticker on the glass. The player never walks to it. They just tap it.

3A new kind of script

Put a LocalScript inside the button

Before, you used a plain Script. Screen buttons need a different one: a LocalScript. A LocalScript is code that runs on the player's own screen. On their phone or their computer, not on the game's backstage brain. That is perfect for buttons, because a button lives on one player's screen.

✅ LocalScript goes INSIDE the button

🔘 TextButton
📜 LocalScript
Your turn
  1. In the Explorer, put your mouse on the TextButton.
  2. Click the + sign.
  3. Choose LocalScript.
  4. Check the LocalScript sits inside the TextButton.
  5. Double click it to open it. Delete everything inside.

Same idea as before: the note goes inside the thing it belongs to. 🐤

4Write the code

When the button is tapped, print a message

Now the code, one small piece at a time. First tell the code which button is yours. Type this line:

NEW LINE
local button = script.Parent

Read it like a sentence: "script is me, this note. Parent is the thing I am tucked inside, my TextButton. Let us call it button."

Careful: capital P in Parent! The computer thinks parent and Parent are two different words.

Now the WHEN and the DO. A button has an alarm called MouseButton1Click. It rings when the player clicks or taps the button. Add these lines under Line 1:

NEW LINES
button.MouseButton1Click:Connect(function()
    print("You clicked the button!")
end)

In kid language: "Hey button! You have an alarm called MouseButton1Click that rings when someone taps you. When it rings, print a message." The end closes the box of steps.

Your whole LocalScript is now this:

local button = script.Parent

button.MouseButton1Click:Connect(function()
    print("You clicked the button!")
end)

Watch the big word: capital M, capital B, capital C in MouseButton1Click. And it is the number 1, not the letter l.

Your turn
  1. Type all the lines yourself. Go slow.
  2. Read it out loud like a story.

"My button is called button. When it is clicked, print a message."

🍌 BRAIN SNACK: same "when X, do Y" pattern

You already know this! On Day 2, Touched was the alarm for blocks. Now MouseButton1Click is the alarm for buttons. Different alarm, same idea: when it rings, do the steps inside. Every event in the game works this way.

Nothing happened? Tap here, do not panic
  1. Is the LocalScript INSIDE the TextButton? Check the Explorer. It must sit under the button (Step 3), not next to it. If it is in the wrong place, hold the left mouse button on the LocalScript and drag it onto the button, then let go.
  2. Is it a LocalScript, not a plain Script? Buttons need a LocalScript. Look at the icon in the Explorer and make sure.
  3. Look for red text in the Output. Red text points at the line with the problem. Check for a tiny typo: a small letter that should be capital (MouseButton1Click, Connect, Parent), or the letter l where a 1 should be.
6Bonus

Make the button change its OWN words

Printing a message is nice, but the player only sees it in the Output. Let us make the button change the words on itself, so the player sees it too. A button holds its words in Text. Change the print line to this:

CHANGE THIS LINE
    button.Text = "You tapped me!"

It means: "button, change your Text to these new words." Now when the player taps, the button itself changes in front of their eyes.

Capital T in Text. And keep the spaces at the start of the line, so it stays inside the box.

Your turn
  1. Change the print line to the button.Text line.
  2. Press Play.
  3. Tap the button. Do the words change?
  4. Try your own words. Play again.

Now the whole screen answers the player. You built a real button!

🍌 BRAIN SNACK: a LocalScript runs on the player's own screen

A plain Script runs on the game's backstage brain, one for the whole game. A LocalScript runs on one player's screen, their own phone or computer. Buttons and screen things live on each player's screen, so they use LocalScripts. That is the whole rule.
