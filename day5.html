1The big idea

A checkpoint is a save flag

A checkpoint is a spot in your game that the player can save. Think of a long walk to the shops. Halfway, there is a big tree. You say, "If I get tired, I will come back to this tree, not all the way home." The tree is your checkpoint. In a game, when a player touches a checkpoint, the game remembers it as their new start. Fall down after that, and you come back there.

2The right part

Add a SpawnLocation for the flag

Here is the most important rule of the whole day. A checkpoint is NOT a normal Part. It must be a special part called a SpawnLocation. A SpawnLocation is a "start here" pad. Roblox already knows how to bring players back to one. A normal Part cannot do that. So we must use the right kind of part.

Careful: it must be a SpawnLocation, not a plain Part. A normal Part will NOT work as a checkpoint.

4Find the player

When someone touches it, find the player

Open the Script and delete everything in it. First, name our flag. Then listen for a touch. Type these lines:

NEW LINES
local checkpoint = script.Parent

checkpoint.Touched:Connect(function(otherPart)
    local character = otherPart.Parent
    local player = game.Players:GetPlayerFromCharacter(character)

Read it slowly. checkpoint is our flag, this note's Parent. When something touches it, we look at what touched it (otherPart). A player's foot belongs to their body, so otherPart.Parent is the body, called the character. Then GetPlayerFromCharacter asks, "which player owns this body?" It hands us back the player.

Capital letters matter: Touched, Connect, Players, GetPlayerFromCharacter. The computer reads them exactly.

Your turn
  1. Type these four lines. Go slow.
  2. Say what GetPlayerFromCharacter does.

Answer: it finds which player owns this body.

🍌 BRAIN SNACK: A body and its owner are two things

In Roblox, the thing that walks around is the character, the body. The player is the person who owns it. They are not the same. GetPlayerFromCharacter is the bridge: give it a body, it gives you the owner.

5Save the spot

Set this flag as their new start

Now the real magic. We tell the player, "come back to life HERE next time." That spot is called their RespawnLocation. We set it to our checkpoint. Add these lines under the last ones:

NEW LINES
    if player then
        player.RespawnLocation = checkpoint
    end
end)

RespawnLocation is where a player comes back to life. We set it to our checkpoint, so next time they fall, they wake up here. But why the if player check? Sometimes the thing that touches the flag is not a player at all. It could be a falling block or another part. Then GetPlayerFromCharacter gives back nothing. The if player line means "only do this IF we really found a player." It keeps the code safe.

Capital R and L in RespawnLocation. Do not forget the last end) that closes the touch box.

Your turn
  1. Type these lines.
  2. Say why we check if player.

Answer: because sometimes a non-player thing touches the flag.

🍌 BRAIN SNACK: The flag MUST be a SpawnLocation

Say it one more time so you never forget. RespawnLocation can only point at a SpawnLocation. If your flag is a normal Part, this will not work. Right part, right result.

6Play it

The whole script. Add flags. Test it. 🚩

Here is your whole script, all together:

local checkpoint = script.Parent

checkpoint.Touched:Connect(function(otherPart)
    local character = otherPart.Parent
    local player = game.Players:GetPlayerFromCharacter(character)
    if player then
        player.RespawnLocation = checkpoint
    end
end)
